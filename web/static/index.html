Are your germline alleles seen in human populations?

Drop a FASTA of human IGH alleles. Each one is aligned to its gene, and every difference from the reference genome is looked up in 1000 Genomes and in the datasets collected by dbSNP. You get a verdict per allele, the reasons, and the evidence.

Drop a FASTA file here or press Enter to choose one. Names like IGHV1-69*01; names containing _S are judged as novel candidates.
Options
  • Private. The analysis runs in this browser tab. Your sequences are not uploaded.
  • Data on demand. Only the regions of your genes are downloaded: a few MB for J or D genes, roughly 100 MB and several minutes for a full V set. They are kept in this browser, so the next run on the same genes starts almost at once.
  • Without gnomAD ancestry groups. The gnomAD server does not allow access from web pages; its overall frequencies still arrive through dbSNP. The command-line program reads gnomAD in full and is faster.
  • Gene names are checked first. Before anything is downloaded, the page lists the genes of your file. A name it does not know can be matched to a gene, found by sequence or left out, and the gene list itself can be edited.
  • Already ran igpop? Drop its .tsv output files here to browse them.

Gene list:

The gene list says where each gene lies on the reference genome. An allele is matched to a gene by the part of its name before *. Add a gene, remove one, or load your own list (tab-separated: gene, build, contig, start, end, strand). Your list is kept in this browser.
